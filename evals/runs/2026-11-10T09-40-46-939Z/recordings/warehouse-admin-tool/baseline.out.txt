**Key decisions the diagram encodes**

- **Login:** Google OIDC, restricted to your Workspace domain. Roles (e.g. `support`, `support-lead`, `warehouse`) come from Google Groups, so access is managed where you already manage people.
- **Refund limit:** enforced server-side in the Refund service, with the limit tied to role. The UI only hides the button; it is not the control.
- **Audit trail:** the write and its audit record are committed in the same DB transaction, so a change can't exist without a record. The audit table is append-only (no update or delete permissions for the app's DB user).
- **Size:** at 60 users, a single deployable (modular monolith) with one database is enough. No queues or microservices needed.

**Assumptions to confirm**

- Orders already live in another system (e-commerce platform or ERP). If so, "App DB" becomes a cache plus local refund/audit data, and that system is the source of truth for address changes.
- Refunds go through an existing payment provider.
- Warehouse staff may only need lookup and address changes, not refunds.

If any of those are wrong, I can adjust the diagram.
